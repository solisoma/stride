# Stride — Learning Rules

These rules guide the implemented account-based learning loop. Supported, independent, and delayed practice are tracked; source decisions affect future context. Complete syllabus mapping, structured hints, external fact verification, and live quality evaluation remain pending.

## Focused guides
- Organize guides by learning objectives.
- Include essential concepts, prerequisites, examples, and common misconceptions.
- Cite the original source and location; generated notes are not authoritative sources.
- Map every supplied syllabus objective to a guide or flag missing material.
- Without a syllabus, label coverage as provisional.

## Questions
- Support multiple-choice, typed-answer, and mixed practice.
- Test application through scenarios, with difficulty appropriate to the learner.
- Store answer criteria and supporting evidence before presenting a question.
- Keep scenarios answerable from the supplied material and stated assumptions.
- Use plausible multiple-choice distractors with clear explanations.
- In mixed interview practice, avoid revealing the intended algorithm pattern.

## Evaluation and correction
- Judge concepts and reasoning rather than exact wording, spelling, or grammar.
- Accept valid alternative approaches.
- Ask for clarification when an answer is ambiguous.
- Explain what was correct, the specific gap, why it matters, and how to improve.
- Support factual corrections with verified source references.
- If evidence is missing or conflicting, state the limitation instead of inventing a verdict.
- Let learners challenge feedback and record revised evaluations.

## Pasted code
Review the approach, likely bugs, edge cases, and time/space complexity. Do not execute code in the MVP. Describe a successful review as apparent correctness, not tested correctness. Offer suggested tests and a revised explanation where useful.

## Progress and reassessment
Track objective coverage separately from performance. Record first independent attempts, attempts with hints, and answers after correction separately. Use fresh scenarios after feedback and later reassessment to check retention. A single correct answer does not establish mastery; progress labels should describe observed evidence.

## Choosing the next step
Prioritize missing prerequisites, repeated weak concepts, and objectives not yet practised. Account for the learner's goal and preferences. Explain each recommendation briefly. Allow the learner to choose another topic or format.

## Inclusion and trust
Use clear language and manageable feedback lengths. Preserve access to multiple-choice practice for learners who find typing difficult. Declare supported languages, keep personal learning information private, and distinguish source-backed requirements from generated learning advice.

## Disputed material
Quote the cited source accurately, separate model suggestions from verified facts, and let the learner keep a source, select an unverified interpretation, exclude a passage, or leave the concern unresolved. Preserve the decision and show uncertainty. Update plans after new uploads/decisions without deleting progress.
