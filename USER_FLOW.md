# Stride — User Flow

This workflow now has an account-based frontend and backend. Learners can resume journeys, add materials, follow topic recommendations, practise, chat, compare source concerns, and revisit saved history. Source checks and topic coverage remain provisional; comprehensive syllabus coverage and structured hints are pending.

## 1. Start a journey
Choose exam preparation, technical interview preparation, or a new field. Enter the subject or role, current experience, and optional target date.

## 2. Add materials
Upload documents and, where available, a syllabus or topic outline. Show processing progress, failures, and retry options. A failed upload must not erase an existing journey.

## 3. Review the learning map
Show sections and objectives derived from the materials. Let the learner correct the map. Distinguish syllabus-backed coverage from a provisional outline inferred from documents.

## 4. Read a focused guide
Present essential concepts, examples, misconceptions, and references. Offer deeper reading and a downloadable guide.

## 5. Practise
Choose multiple-choice, typed-answer, or mixed mode. Present one scenario at a time. Technical questions can accept pasted code and an explanation. Offer hints, recording their use separately from independent attempts.

## 6. Receive feedback
Explain what worked, what was mistaken, why, and which concept needs revision. Include supporting references. For ambiguous answers, ask a clarification. Allow feedback to be challenged.

## 7. Strengthen a weak concept
Offer a targeted explanation or prerequisite guide, then a fresh scenario. Never count a repeated answer immediately after correction as independent mastery.

## 8. Continue or return later
Save progress and recommend a specific next activity with a short reason. Revisit earlier concepts in later sessions. For interviews, include mixed questions without revealing the algorithm pattern.

## Limited connectivity
Downloaded guides remain readable offline. Interactive generation requires connectivity in the MVP. Preserve submitted attempts and provide retry behavior without duplicating them.

## Completion
Show covered, unpractised, and revision-needed objectives. Describe readiness using observed practice evidence; do not guarantee exam results or interview success.

## Account and source decisions
Sign in to resume saved journeys. New uploads update the plan and preserve history. Source concerns show the source quote and an unverified alternative; learners choose how to use them without the system declaring their preference factual verification.

## Unified learning workspace
Practice and follow-up chat share one Learn workspace: scenarios, answers, and correction sit beside the saved conversation, with a stacked layout on small screens. Asking about a mistake prefills and focuses the chat composer without leaving the scenario. Dashboard navigation includes real resource/attempt/topic counts, recent activity, a learning path, and the next recommendation; no progress metrics are fabricated.
